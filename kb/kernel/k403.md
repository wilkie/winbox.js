## Observed behaviour

- [[read out]] `KRNL386.EXE` seg1 `29f1`-`2a00`. Two words, a selector and the new owner, four bytes popped (the selector is the first pushed, at BP+8). It calls seg1 `2a03`, which finds the block's arena as [[fn:KERNEL.K404]] does and writes the owner to its word at 12h (`2a13`-`2a1c`). Nothing is answered; AX is kept.
- [[read out]] `KRNL286.EXE` seg1 `26a9` writes the word at 1 of the arena a paragraph before the block (seg1 `26bc`-`26cb`).
- [[read out]] KERNEL uses it while it makes a task. The new task's database is made its own owner: seg2 `2160`-`2164` calls `29f1` with the database's selector as both arguments.
- Not yet measured.

## Callers

- No file of the installations or the corpus imports it or holds its name (`kb/data/kernel-callers.json`).

## Implementation

A stub in both engines.

**Recommendation: implement together with [[fn:KERNEL.K404]] if ever; low priority.** Writing an owner into WinBox's record of a block is a few lines. But a program that moved its blocks' owners would also change what [[fn:KERNEL.GlobalFreeAll]] and a task's end free, so it wants a probe first. See [[topic:kernel-internals]].
