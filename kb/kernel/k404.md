## Observed behaviour

- [[read out]] `KRNL386.EXE` seg1 `29ae`-`29bc`. One word, a selector, popped. It calls KERNEL's own routine at seg1 `29bf`. That routine finds the block's arena through the selector table, entry selector ÷ 8 of the table whose length is at 22Eh and whose linear address is at 230h ([[fn:KERNEL.THHOOK]], routine seg1 `297c`). It answers the arena's word at 12h, or nought where the selector is past the table.
- [[read out]] `KRNL286.EXE` seg1 `267f` does the same over its own arenas, a paragraph before the block, where the owner is the word at 1 (seg1 `2690`-`26a0`).
- [[read out]] The owner is what KERNEL's own code asks for: [[fn:KERNEL.HasGPHandler]] and [[fn:KERNEL.PatchCodeHandle]] ask it of a code segment to find its module (seg1 `4c7b`, `7bce`).
- Not yet measured. A probe could ask it, through `GetProcAddress` and ordinal 404, of its own data segment, a block it allocated and USER's data. The answers would show which of module, task or prefix each block is charged to.

## Callers

- No file of the installations or the corpus imports it or holds its name (`kb/data/kernel-callers.json`). Its partner is [[fn:KERNEL.K403]].

## Implementation

A stub in both engines, which pops the word and leaves AX as it was.

**Recommendation: implement as a fixed answer if a probe records it; low priority.** WinBox knows who owns each of its blocks (TOOLHELP's `GlobalEntry` already answers an owner, [[topic:tool-helper]]), so the answer is a lookup. That is about 15 lines in each engine, plus the probe above. See [[topic:kernel-internals]].
